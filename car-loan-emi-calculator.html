Car Loan EMI Calculator

Enter the car price, down payment, interest rate and tenure to see your monthly car loan EMI, total interest and total cost.

How it works

The calculator subtracts your down payment from the car price to get the loan amount, then applies the standard EMI formula: EMI = L x r x (1+r)^n / ((1+r)^n - 1), where L is the loan, r is the monthly rate and n is the number of months.

A bigger down payment or a shorter tenure lowers the total interest you pay. Interest rates, fees and insurance differ by bank, so use the result as an estimate and confirm the final figures with your bank or leasing company.

Example: how tenure changes your EMI

Take a car priced at Rs. 4,000,000 with a 30% down payment, which leaves a loan of Rs. 2,800,000. At an example rate of 15% a year (your bank's rate may differ), the tenure changes both the monthly payment and the total interest.

How to choose a plan

  • Pick the shortest tenure whose EMI fits comfortably in your monthly budget.
  • A larger down payment is the simplest way to cut interest.
  • Ask the bank about processing fees, insurance and early-settlement charges, since they are not part of this estimate.

The rates in these tables are only examples to show how the numbers move. Always use the rate your bank quotes.

Frequently asked questions

How much down payment should I make on a car?

A larger down payment means a smaller loan and less interest. Many lenders ask for at least 10% to 30%, but the requirement depends on the bank.

Does the EMI include insurance and fees?

No. This estimate covers only the loan amount, interest rate and tenure. Processing fees and insurance are extra.

Is a longer tenure better?

A longer tenure lowers your monthly EMI but increases the total interest. Pick the shortest tenure you can comfortably afford.

Does a bigger down payment lower my EMI?

Yes. A larger down payment reduces the loan amount, so both the EMI and the total interest go down.

Can I use this for a bike or used car loan?

Yes. Enter the price, down payment, rate and tenure of that loan and the same formula applies.
