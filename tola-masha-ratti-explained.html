Tola, Masha and Ratti: Gold Weight Units Explained

Learn how tola, masha and ratti relate to each other and to grams and ounces, with simple examples for gold and silver.

Gold and silver in Pakistan are still weighed in traditional units: the tola, the masha and the ratti. If you buy jewellery, check prices or work out zakat, you need to know how these units compare with grams and ounces.

The units

  • 1 tola is about 11.66 grams (11.6638 g).
  • 1 tola is 12 masha.
  • 1 masha is 8 ratti, or about 0.972 grams.
  • 1 ratti is about 0.1215 grams.
  • 1 tola is 96 ratti.

Tola and the international ounce

Global gold prices use the troy ounce, which is 31.1035 grams. One troy ounce is about 2.667 tola. Dividing the ounce price by 2.667 gives a rough per-tola figure, but local premiums, making charges and purity change what you actually pay.

Example

If a bracelet weighs 35 grams, it is about 3 tola (35 divided by 11.66 equals 3.0). A ring that weighs 4.8 grams is roughly 5 masha.

Why the numbers can differ slightly

Some shops use 11.66 grams for a tola and others use 11.664 grams. The gap is small for one tola, but it adds up on bigger purchases, so check the digital scale and ask which value the shop uses.

Tola and zakat

Many people measure the zakat nisab in tola, using 52.5 tola of silver or 7.5 tola of gold. Scholars differ on the method, so ask a qualified scholar which one applies to you.
